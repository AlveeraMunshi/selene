A lunar rover that replans
because the map was wrong

SELENE plans routes with A* over a belief the rover builds from its own sensors, then drives them across terrain it cannot fully see. Every replan, every rejected route and every number behind the choice stays open to inspection.

The problem

Most planning demos cannot replan, because nothing surprises them

Hand a planner the true hazard map and it will produce one good route and never need another. The replanning, the alternatives and the explanations are then decoration: there is no moment where the world disagrees with the plan.

SELENE splits the terrain into two layers. One is visible from orbit and goes into the planner. One is discoverable only by the rover's own sensors, at twenty four metres, and the planner is never shown it. The route is built from the first layer and driven against both, so it fails honestly and has to be rebuilt in flight.

What it actually does

The planner is never shown the answer

Hazard is split into an orbital layer and a sensor only layer. A route that looked clear can be invalidated at twenty four metres and has to be rebuilt against what the rover now believes.

Optimality is proved, not asserted

A* returns the exact uniform cost optimum on 160 generated terrains across four risk settings. Maximum cost gap 0.0, while expanding 32 to 49 percent of the oracle's nodes.

Explanations come from the decision

Prose is generated from the structured record, never written beside it, so the sentence on screen cannot drift away from the arithmetic that produced the route.

The rover stops and asks

When the only feasible route exceeds the risk you set, the mission holds and offers real alternatives with their real costs. The request is a value in the state, so nothing blocks and the mission still replays.

A mission replays exactly

The record stores inputs and never frames, so a replay runs the engine again from the seed and reproduces the event log byte for byte. Anything that leaked a clock into a decision shows up here.

The numbers come from a benchmark

540 missions across seeds and parameter grids, medians with spread, and a reproducibility gate in CI over seven fixed missions that fails on a single changed digest.

Give a planner the whole map and it never has to think again. The interesting part is what it does without one.

How it works

Step one

Plan on belief

A* over an eight connected grid with four weighted terms: distance, slope, believed hazard and predicted energy. Corner cutting is forbidden, and the heuristic is a provable lower bound on cost per cell.

Step two

Drive and sense

The rover reveals hidden hazard inside its sensor radius as it moves, folding it into the belief. Battery, distance and energy are drawn down by a model that charges more for climbing than for flat ground.

Step three

Replan, or hold

A material rise in hazard on the active route triggers a new search against several postures. If nothing feasible clears the risk bar, the mission holds for an operator instead of guessing.

Measured, including what did not work

Results from the benchmark, not from the README

0.0

Maximum absolute cost gap between A* and a uniform cost oracle, over 160 terrains and four risk tolerances. Zero violations. This is the claim the whole project rests on and it is a differential test, not an assertion about the code.

−51% to −60%

Reduction in mean hazard from risk aware planning against a shortest distance baseline, for 0.8 to 3.8 percent more distance. Lower on 33 of 33 paired seeds.

8 of 12

Missions completed by the risk aware planner where the distance dominant one completed 11 of 12. Caution costs completion rate, and the distance penalty grows to 24.4 percent once the loop is closed.

0.2% to 0.5%

Median energy saved across the whole range of the energy preference, which makes it a weak control. The cause is physical: net elevation is fixed by the endpoints and the climb term is only 12 to 19 percent of route energy.

317 + 29

Unit and integration tests, plus end to end tests in a real browser. Two consecutive full runs produced identical results and eighty seven repeat runs found no flakes.

Every figure above came from a run that was performed. The two amber rows are findings that argue against the design, kept because a benchmark that only flatters the system is not a benchmark.

What this is not

This is a simulation, and the gaps are stated rather than implied

The terrain is procedural noise, not elevation data. The energy model is linear in slope with no regolith mechanics, no thermal and no regenerative braking. Hazard severity is a scalar with no failure probability behind it, so a risk tolerance of 0.45 is not calibrated against anything physical. The sensor is a perfect disc with no occlusion and no noise.

The largest departure from a real mission is that the rover always knows exactly where it is. There is no localisation error at all. Fifteen more limitations are written out in the research document, along with the seven measurements worth making next.

Questions

The things an engineer asks first

Is the rover following a path that was computed in advance?

No. Every route comes from an A* search run at the moment it is needed, over a belief state that is strictly poorer than the simulator's ground truth. When a hazard is discovered on the active route, the search runs again against several cost postures and the alternatives it rejected are kept so you can read them.

How do I know the explanations are not just text?

Each decision writes a structured record holding every candidate that was actually evaluated, its per term costs, the selected option and the caveats. The sentence you read is generated from that record, and the record is shown underneath it. A test asserts that every numeral in the generated prose matches a value derived from the record.

Does changing the risk setting really change anything?

Yes, and it is measured rather than claimed. On the rille crossing scenario, a risk tolerance of 0.15 produces a 752 metre route at peak hazard 0.27, and 0.85 produces a 483 metre route at peak hazard 0.75. The energy preference, by contrast, barely moves the route, and the results section says so.

Why is there no language model in the decision path?

Because the reasoning has to stay inspectable arithmetic. A model may one day render a record more fluently, but it must never be the thing that makes the choice, or every guarantee on this page becomes unenforceable. The deterministic trace is the source of truth and the explanation layer sits on top of it.

How is reproducibility enforced?

Nothing in the engine may read the clock, call the global random number generator or touch the DOM. Time is a tick counter and randomness is a seeded stream. A gate in CI replays seven fixed missions covering six outcome classes and fails on a single changed digest. The gate itself was verified by corrupting a digest and watching it exit non zero.

What was hardest to get right?

Scoring the alternatives honestly. A* already returns the optimum under the mission's weights, so re costing rejected routes under those same weights makes the chosen one win by construction and the comparison carries no information. Selection charges a term the search cannot optimise, peak hazard above tolerance, which is a maximum over the path rather than a sum over steps.

Do I need an account, or to install anything?

No. It runs entirely in the browser, saves missions to local storage on your own machine, and sends nothing anywhere. The source is public and the benchmark runs from a single command.

What would you build next?

Thread search effort through to the mission metrics, which currently makes every node count open loop. Then characterise the one failure nobody can explain: a single seed that drives a kilometre, replans seventy six times and then has no route left.
